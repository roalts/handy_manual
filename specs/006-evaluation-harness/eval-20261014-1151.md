- step: 3. Ask the site whether there was a power event near the trip, such as a supply dip or outage, a generator transfer, or work in the site switchgear, because a confirmed supply dip is the most common harmless cause.
- step: 4. Notify the site that you will isolate the compressor, normally for 60 to 90 minutes, agree when it can go down, and record the name of the person notified and the agreed time.
- step: 5. Isolate the compressor at its main isolator, apply LOTO per HV-PROC-02, and isolate and lock any separately fed control or heater supply if the asset isolation sheet lists one.

Question: How do I recover and recharge the refrigerant on an NK-600?

- warning: Never top up an NK-600 circuit to clear a low suction pressure alarm: a short charge means the circuit has a leak, and if you cannot find the cause you must stop and escalate.
- statement: If your NK-600 serial is in NK600-220001 to NK600-231999 and the unit has L-03 after an idle period, check the EXV position in the service menu before you leak test or add refrigerant, because a sticking expansion valve gives the same alarm as a low charge and adding refrigerant does not correct it.
- warning: Wear safety glasses and cryogenic gloves, ventilate plant rooms before work, and isolate the unit at the main switch before reaching into the fan section because refrigerant can cause frostbite, enclosed-space asphyxiation, and live fan/EXV driver terminals can remain energized for up to 3 minutes after isolation.
- step: 1. Check the asset serial number against open service bulletins and campaigns for the model, then read the alarm history on the controller and note which circuit alarmed, when, and the suction and discharge pressures at the time.
- step: 2. Check for non-leak causes of low suction pressure: an iced evaporator, low chilled water flow, a dirty condenser coil, a blocked filter drier if the temperature drop across the drier is above 3 C, or an expansion valve not modulating.
- step: 3. Inspect for oil stains at joints, flare nuts, service valves, and the compressor, because oil marks the place where refrigerant escapes.
- step: 4. Run the unit and leak test both circuits with a calibrated electronic leak detector with sensitivity 3 g/year or better, moving the probe at no more than 5 cm per second below joints because R-513A is heavier than air.
- step: 5. Confirm every detector indication with bubble solution before opening the circuit.
- step: 6. If no leak is found but the charge is low, do not top up; record the result, tag the unit, and escalate to the team lead for an ultrasonic or tracer-gas test because the lost refrigerant went somewhere.
- step: 7. Recover the refrigerant from the affected circuit into a labelled recovery cylinder and weigh and record the recovered amount.
- step: 8. Repair the leak, and replace the filter drier core 63-0410 whenever the circuit has been opened.
- step: 9. Fit the gauge manifold and nitrogen regulator, and never pressurise directly from the nitrogen cylinder without a regulator.
- step: 10. Pressurise in steps: first to 3 bar and check for audible leaks, then to 10 bar and check with bubble solution, and then pressurise with dry nitrogen to 15 bar and hold for 30 minutes while recording the start and end pressure and ambient temperature at start and end.

Question: I need to clean the foot strainer in pump pit PP-1 at Fresno. How do I enter the pit safely?

- statement: PP-1 is a permit-required confined space raw water pit at Fresno, and you enter it for foot strainer cleaning, suction valve work, or suction line leaks; do not reach into PP-1 through the open hatch with your head below deck level, because that is entry.
- warning: Before you go, set Required certification to Confined space on the work order and get supervisor approval with both names assigned, because entry is only allowed under the controlled confined-space procedure.
- step: 1. Ask Sierra Springs EHS for the site confined space permit at least 48 hours ahead.
- step: 2. Check the gas monitor is in calibration and bump test it on the day of the entry before leaving for site.
- step: 3. Check the rescue equipment: tripod, winch, full-body harness, retrieval line, and ventilation blower with ducting.
- warning: For PP-1, use non-entry retrieval only; the tripod must be centred over the 900 mm hatch with the winch on the leg facing the pump house door, and if a straight lift is impossible and no second anchor can be set, the entry does not go ahead.
- step: 4. Electrically isolate and lock every pump that discharges into, draws from, or stands in the pit, and for PP-1 close and lock raw water supply valve V-101 and the suction isolation valve.
- step: 5. Drain the suction line to the pit before entry, and record every lock on the permit.
- step: 6. Test the air before anyone breaks the plane of the hatch by sampling from outside at three levels: just below the hatch, at mid-depth, and 0.3 m above the floor, holding the tube at each level for at least two minutes.
- statement: For Fresno pits, acceptable entry air is oxygen 19.5% to 23.5%, hydrogen sulphide below 10 ppm, carbon monoxide below 25 ppm, flammable gas below 10% LEL, and carbon dioxide below 5,000 ppm.
- warning: If any reading is outside those limits, ventilate and test again; if the readings are still outside the limits after 30 minutes of ventilation, do not enter, close the hatch, inform Sierra Springs EHS, and call the entry supervisor and the team lead.
- step: 7. Run the ventilation blower with the duct to 0.5 m above the pit floor for at least 10 minutes before the final pre-entry test, keep it running for the whole entry, and place the blower inlet upwind and away from the truck yard.
- warning: Do not use pure oxygen or compressed air from the plant system to ventilate.
- step: 8. When the readings are acceptable, the entry supervisor completes and signs the Corvane permit and posts it at the hatch next to the site permit.
- statement: The permit is valid for one shift, at most 8 hours, and only for the work and people named on it; if everyone is out of the pit for more than 30 minutes, you need a new pre-entry test.
- step: 9. For the entry, wear a full-body harness with the retrieval line attached to the dorsal ring, keep the personal gas monitor in the breathing zone, and keep the line attached to the winch for the whole entry.
- step: 10. The attendant logs you in with the time, and you and the attendant speak at least every five minutes while the attendant watches the blower, the monitor display where visible, and the yard.
