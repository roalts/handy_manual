- warning: Never top up an NK-600 circuit to clear a low suction pressure alarm; if you cannot find the cause, stop and escalate.
- warning: If your NK-600 serial number is in campaign NK-C25-14 and the unit has an L-03 alarm, check the EXV position in the service menu before you add refrigerant, because a sticking expansion valve can give the same alarm as a low charge and refrigerant will not fix it.

- step: 1. Check the unit serial number against open service bulletins and campaigns for the model before you start.
- step: 2. Read the alarm history on the controller and note which circuit alarmed, when it alarmed, and the suction and discharge pressures at the time.
- step: 3. Check for non-leak causes of low suction pressure: an iced evaporator, low chilled water flow, a dirty condenser coil, a blocked filter drier with a temperature drop across the drier above 3 C, or an expansion valve not modulating.
- step: 4. Inspect for oil stains at joints, flare nuts, service valves, and the compressor, because oil marks the place where refrigerant escapes.
- step: 5. Run the unit and leak test both circuits with a calibrated electronic leak detector with sensitivity 3 g/year or better, moving the probe at no more than 5 cm per second below joints because R-513A is heavier than air.
- step: 6. Confirm every indication with bubble solution before you open the circuit.
- step: 7. If no leak is found but the charge is low, do not top up; record the result, tag the unit, and escalate to the team lead for an ultrasonic or tracer-gas test.
- step: 8. Recover the refrigerant from the affected circuit into a labelled recovery cylinder and weigh and record the recovered amount.
- step: 9. Repair the leak, and replace the filter drier core 63-0410 whenever the circuit has been opened.
- step: 10. Fit the gauge manifold and nitrogen regulator, and never pressurize directly from the nitrogen cylinder without a regulator.
- step: 11. Pressurize in steps to 3 bar and check for audible leaks, then to 10 bar and check with bubble solution, then pressurize with dry nitrogen to 15 bar and hold for 30 minutes.
- step: 12. Assess the pressure-test result against Appendix C, and record the start and end pressure and the ambient temperature at start and end because nitrogen pressure changes with temperature.
- step: 13. Release the nitrogen to atmosphere outdoors.
- step: 14. Connect a vacuum pump with short, large-bore hoses, remove the Schrader cores for faster evacuation, evacuate to below 500 microns, then isolate the pump and hold for 15 minutes.

- warning: You must not enter until confined-space requirements are in place: set Required certification to Confined space on the work order, get supervisor approval with both names assigned, ask Sierra Springs EHS for the site confined-space permit at least 48 hours ahead, check the gas monitor calibration and bump test it on the day of entry, and check the rescue equipment: tripod, winch, full-body harness, retrieval line, and ventilation blower with ducting.
- warning: For PP-1, non-entry retrieval must be possible for every entry; the tripod must be centred over the 900 mm hatch with the winch on the leg facing the pump house door, and if a straight lift is impossible and no second anchor can be set, the entry does not go ahead.
- step: 1. Electrically isolate and lock every pump that discharges into, draws from, or stands in the pit; for PP-1, close and lock the raw water supply valve V-101 and the suction isolation valve, then drain the suction line to the pit before entry.
- step: 2. Record every lock on the permit.
- step: 3. Test the atmosphere before anyone breaks the plane of the hatch by opening the sample port, or lifting the hatch only far enough for the sample tube, and testing from outside at three levels: just below the hatch, at mid-depth, and 0.3 m above the floor.
- step: 4. Hold the tube at each level for at least two minutes so the pump draws a full sample, because gases layer and a reading at the top tells you nothing about the floor.
- step: 5. Use the five-gas monitor with CO2 sensor, and do not enter unless O2 is 19.5% to 23.5%, H2S is below 10 ppm, CO is below 25 ppm, LEL is below 10% LEL, and CO2 is below 5,000 ppm.
