Question: How do I recover and recharge the refrigerant on an NK-600?

- warning: Never top up an NK-600 circuit just to clear a low suction pressure alarm; if you cannot find the cause, stop and escalate, because adding refrigerant without fixing the cause is not an approved repair and a circuit short of charge has a leak.
- statement: If your unit is in the SB-2025-014 serial range and has L-03, check the EXV position in the service menu before you add refrigerant, because a sticking expansion valve can give the same alarm as a low charge and adding refrigerant will overcharge the circuit when the valve frees again.
- step: 1. Check the asset serial number against open service bulletins and campaigns for the model before you connect gauges.
- step: 2. Read the alarm history on the controller and note which circuit alarmed, when it alarmed, and the suction and discharge pressures at the time.
- step: 3. Check for non-leak causes of low suction pressure: an iced evaporator, low chilled water flow, a dirty condenser coil, a blocked filter drier with temperature drop across the drier above 3 C, or an expansion valve not modulating.
- step: 4. Inspect for oil stains at joints, flare nuts, service valves and the compressor, because oil marks the place where refrigerant escapes.
- step: 5. Run the unit and leak test both circuits with a calibrated electronic leak detector with sensitivity 3 g/year or better, moving the probe at no more than 5 cm per second below joints because R-513A is heavier than air.
- step: 6. Confirm every detector indication with bubble solution before you open the circuit.
- step: 7. If no leak is found but the charge is low, do not top up; record the result, tag the unit, and escalate to the team lead for an ultrasonic or tracer-gas test.
- step: 8. Recover the refrigerant from the affected circuit into a labelled recovery cylinder and weigh and record the recovered amount.
- step: 9. Repair the leak, and replace the filter drier core 63-0410 whenever the circuit has been opened.
- step: 10. Fit the gauge manifold and nitrogen regulator, then pressure test with dry nitrogen in steps of 3 bar and 10 bar, and at 15 bar hold for 30 minutes while recording the start and end pressure and the ambient temperature at start and end.
- warning: Never pressurise directly from the nitrogen cylinder without a regulator, and never use oxygen or compressed air for the strength and tightness test.
- step: 11. Release the nitrogen to atmosphere outdoors, then evacuate the circuit to below 500 microns and isolate the pump for 15 minutes; if the vacuum rises above 1,000 microns, return to the pressure-test stage because there is moisture or a leak.
- step: 12. Charge by weight to the nameplate charge of the circuit as liquid through the liquid line service valve or access port, then start the circuit and check suction pressure, discharge pressure, superheat and subcooling against the manufacturer's values, and leak test the repaired joint again with the unit running.
- step: 13. Label the unit with the refrigerant type, the total charge and the date, and record the quantities, the leak location and the repair in the service record and the operator's F-gas log.
- statement: For NK-600 units in NK-C25-14, replace the EXV on both circuits with 63-1180 rev 3 and the filter drier cores 63-0410, and fit the new stepper cable, because the rev 2 valve can stick partly closed after idle periods and the wrong EXV type setting opens the valve too slowly.

- step: 3. Electrically isolate and lock every pump that discharges into, draws from, or stands in the pit; for PP-1, close and lock the raw water supply valve V-101 and the suction isolation valve, then drain the suction line to the pit before entry.
- step: 4. Record every lock on the permit.
- step: 5. Test before anyone breaks the plane of the hatch, and test from outside by opening the hatch sample port or lifting the hatch only far enough for the sample tube.
- step: 6. Take gas readings at three levels: just below the hatch, at mid-depth, and 0.3 m above the floor, and hold the tube at each level for at least two minutes so the pump draws a full sample.
- statement: For Fresno pits, acceptable entry limits are O2 19.5% to 23.5%, H2S below 10 ppm, CO below 25 ppm, LEL below 10% LEL, and CO2 below 5,000 ppm; low oxygen at Fresno almost always means CO2 has displaced it, and CO2 and H2S sit at the bottom so a top reading tells you nothing about the floor.
